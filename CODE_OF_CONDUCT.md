# Code of Conduct

## Why this exists

Cairn is building moderation and safety tooling. Running a project community that tolerates
harassment while shipping anti-harassment features would be an obvious contradiction — and
a warning about how seriously to take the rest of our claims.

## The standard

Participate in a way that makes other people want to keep participating.

**Expected:**

- Assume good faith; ask before concluding someone is arguing in bad faith
- Disagree with the argument, not the person
- Accept that people have different expertise, and that "obvious" usually is not
- Take responsibility when you get something wrong, fix it, and move on

**Not acceptable:**

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption
- Personal attacks; demeaning comments about people rather than ideas
- Discriminatory language or conduct, including on the basis of gender, gender identity,
  sexual orientation, disability, appearance, race, ethnicity, age, religion, or
  nationality
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission
- Unwelcome sexual attention or imagery
- Advocating for or encouraging any of the above

## Security-specific expectations

- **Disclose vulnerabilities responsibly.** See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
- **Do not use the project's spaces to coordinate attacks** on anyone, including our
  competitors.
- **Criticism of the design is welcome and wanted**, including harsh criticism. "This
  threat model is wrong and here is why" is a contribution. "You are an idiot" is not.

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces — repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, chat — and
when representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Report to the project maintainers via GitHub. Reports are handled confidentially. If a
report concerns a maintainer, it may be sent to any other maintainer.

Responses are proportionate: a private word, a public correction, a temporary ban, or a
permanent ban. We will say what happened where doing so does not expose a reporter.

**Reporting in good faith will never itself be treated as a violation**, even if the report
turns out to be mistaken.
